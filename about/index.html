About CSA

A different kind of learning experience.

Community STE(A)M Academy - Xenia is Ohio's first independent, public STEAM-designated school: tuition-free, lottery-based, and open to all Ohio residents. We opened in Fall 2023 and are gradually building out toward serving grades K-12.

What STEAM means at CSA

The Institute for the Future estimates that roughly 85% of the jobs today's students will hold don't exist yet. Educating for that future takes more than isolated lessons in science, technology, engineering, arts, or math taught one at a time. STEAM at CSA means merging all five into real, project-based learning, so students engage with actual community concerns instead of memorizing for a test.

Our curriculum covers all required subjects while building 21st-century skills, linking learning directly to the community, and prioritizing "learning HOW to think, not WHAT to think" through Workforce Direct, college and career readiness programs, and innovative, hands-on curriculum.

Mission & vision

What guides CSA

CSA-Xenia's culture and priorities come from its Vision, Mission, Purpose, and Core Values: an active, brain-based, student-first model where every learner gets voice and choice in how they demonstrate mastery, through project-based learning linked to real community enterprises, aimed at holistic preparation for college, career, and citizenship.

Mission

To educate the whole student through innovative, student-centered, relevant learning experiences that meet our community's current and future needs, by embracing the rigorous, interdisciplinary, problem-solving STEM pathway while embedding the creativity of the Arts.

Vision

An exemplary educational community that supports all students to engage their heart, mind, body, and soul, becoming academically, socially, emotionally, and physically successful, and ready for service to local and global communities.

Real student work

Lunar Recovery Mission: High Angle Recovery of Personnel and Equipment

In partnership with the Wright Brothers Institute (WBI) and the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL), CSA-Xenia students worked on a real initiative connected to NASA's Artemis program: designing rescue solutions for astronauts and equipment on the lunar surface near Shackleton Crater at the lunar south pole.

  • Every CSA-Xenia student took part in the initial Design Thinking phase; interested students then joined one of three Design Teams
  • Teams researched commercial lunar surface vehicles, analyzed winch performance requirements, and explored robotic arms and unmanned recovery systems
  • Students built Unreal Engine simulations, including a live zip-line recovery demonstration, plus CAD and Blender component models
  • The project culminated in student presentations to WBI and MSgt Ben Johnis, who is actively involved in the real Artemis mission

Our sprint coach later reflected on the value of mentorship and a growth mindset: the student-led teams showed real resilience, creativity, and collaboration under a tight timeline.
